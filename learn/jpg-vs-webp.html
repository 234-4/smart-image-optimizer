JPG vs WebP: Which Image Format Is Better for Websites?

JPG is familiar and dependable. WebP is usually smaller and more flexible. The better choice depends on the image, delivery pipeline and browsers you need to support.

JPG at a glance

JPG is a lossy format designed for continuous-tone images such as photographs. It is supported almost everywhere and works well when you need a predictable export. Its weaknesses are repeated re-saving, visible block artifacts at low quality and no transparency.

Why WebP is often better

WebP supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation. For many photographs it produces a smaller file at similar visual quality. Smaller downloads can reduce transfer time, especially on mobile connections.

Rule of thumb: use WebP as the default web export when your delivery stack supports it, and keep the original JPG as your editing source.

When JPG still wins

Choose JPG when a third-party system only accepts it, when you need the broadest legacy compatibility, or when a particular photo looks cleaner at the same file size. Format labels are not a substitute for comparing actual exports.

What about transparency?

WebP can preserve transparent backgrounds; JPG cannot. For logos, cut-out products and interface assets, WebP or PNG avoids the solid background and edge fringing that a JPG export can introduce.

How to decide

  1. Photograph with no transparency: test WebP first, then JPG.
  2. Graphic with transparency: use WebP or PNG.
  3. Legacy integration: use JPG and set a sensible quality level.
  4. Any important asset: compare the final file and rendered appearance.
