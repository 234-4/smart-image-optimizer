WebP vs AVIF: Which Is Better?

AVIF can produce exceptionally small files, while WebP is easier to encode and broadly established. Your best choice depends on the delivery setup and asset type.

WebP strengths

WebP supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation in one modern format. It has strong browser support and is practical for everyday image pipelines. Encoding is usually straightforward and tooling is widely available.

AVIF strengths

AVIF is based on the AV1 image format and can deliver excellent compression, particularly for photographs and complex images. At the same visual quality, it may be smaller than WebP. It also supports transparency and high color depth.

The trade-offs

AVIF encoding can take longer and high-quality exports may require more processing. Support is strong in modern browsers but older environments still need a fallback. A smaller file is valuable only if your server, CDN and client can deliver it reliably.

Recommended setup: offer AVIF first with WebP or JPG as a fallback, using the HTML `picture` element or an image service that negotiates formats.

Which should you use?

  • Choose AVIF when bandwidth savings are important and your pipeline can encode and cache it.
  • Choose WebP for a dependable modern default with simpler tooling.
  • Keep a JPG or PNG fallback when broad compatibility is required.
